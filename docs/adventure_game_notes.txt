Balance design
 1. traits and attributes should ideally occur in spectrums, perhaps multiple spectrums.
        specific values across all spectrums should sum or average equally along all the classes.
        Example: Say STRENGTH and DEFENSE are opposing spectrums. The combination of these values should
        be roughly comparable between different classes.
        Simplified concept - there could be a total amount of points to distribute among various attributes..
            Distributing these points in different combinations in two classes should allow entities from those classes,
            assuming similar experience levels and balanced equipment (weapons, spells, special skills (Kung Fu), when
            battling each other to have similar records of wins vs losses. Tool should simulate many such interactions
            to verify this balance.

On-Person Items
1. What the person is wearing on their body to include
    a. clothing
    b. armor
    c. rings

    Wearables are items that are affixed loosely or tightly to a person and require no effort to maintain once
    so worn, like pants, a shirt, boots, armor, rings, etc.
2. Holding
    a. weapons - requires use of hands. Some weapons require only one hand, some require two. If person only has
        one hand, this may limit what weapons they can use.
    b. shields - if attacked to arm may count as wearable, but probably less stable than clothing or regular armor.
                 if larger, requires at least one arm/hand

3. Carrying
    a. Bags or sacks may require one or both hands depending on how heavy the contents are.
    b. a backpack, purse, fanny or front pack may be held with a strap around the legs, waist, shoulders, or
       back, allowing hands-free use of the bag.
    c. Bag can only hold things smaller than themselves, and have a fixed volume. I.e., have limits on what
        can fit inside, as well as overall weight limits for the person.

4. Porters
    a. OG D&D anticipated the need/use of hirelings for combat and to carry supplies. Such a porter may be
        carrying items belonging to a player thus increasing what the player is carrying "in the field."
    b. If a porter runs away the player may lose items, unless the porter abandons them. Even so, if the
        items cannot be distributed to the remaining porters, some items must be left behind.

5. Beats of burden
    a. horses, mules, donkeys, etc, with or without carts, might accompany a party on a quest. These need to
        be tended to so they remain healthy, don't run away or get stolen by wandering brigands.

6. Weight restrictions
    a. This is a fantasy game, so real world weight restrictions don't apply. But there still must be limits?
